# Defect register

The standing list of what the gates could not see, and the gate that now covers each entry. A defect a user reports is fixed *and* gated: an entry stays here after it becomes a gate, because the register is where the next defect comes from. A gate counts only once it has been made to go red by reintroducing the defect — that evidence is recorded with the entry.

Format per entry: **symptom** (what a person saw) · **investigation** (what was measured) · **root cause** · **gate** (the check that covers the class, and its red proof) · **bounds** (what the gate does not cover).

## 2026-09-17 — a whole feature was missing, and every gate was green (found by audit, not reported)

**Symptom.** Nothing looked broken. The model is the US variant (its non-goals say "no SIM tray (US eSIM)"), and a US iPhone 17 Pro carries a **5G mmWave antenna window centred on its top edge** — a matte rounded-rectangle insert about 40 % of the body's width. The model's top edge had only the microphone bore, and the `top` view preset frames exactly that surface. Seven rounds of screenshots had looked straight at it.

**Investigation.** Found by running the viewer and asking what belongs on each surface, with a reference photo in hand rather than by reading code. `.shots/ref/mmwave-1.jpg` (an end-on top-edge shot, provenance in `docs/work/000_iphone-viewer/plan.md`) measures the window at 267 px of a 663 px edge, centred, covering 42 px of the face's 71 px depth; its own insert reads 0.81 of the frame's luma on the same row and is flat across its face (p5/p50/p95 = 109/110/112) where the frame runs 104→165.

**Root cause.** An invariant gate can only bound parts that exist; nothing in the suite can see an absence. The suite was strong exactly where it was pointed — 32 tests over the parts that were there — and the missing feature sat on a surface no audit had compared feature-by-feature against a reference.

**Gate.** `parts.test.ts`, `describe("the top edge's mmWave antenna window")` — four tests that build the part inside each test (so a missing window fails by name rather than reporting "no tests") and **re-derive the placement from the reference photo's own pixel literals** (663/267/71/42) rather than reading the implementation's constants, so retuning the part cannot redefine the check. Red proofs, all reverted: the part removed → all four fail with `the built model has no part named "mmwave-window"`; moved 2.4 mm off centre; pulled 0.9 mm off the edge face; deepened to 8 mm (breaks the depth margin); widened 6 mm (breaks the width fraction *and* the mic-bore clearance). A fifth gate pins the insert's own material character in `materials.test.ts` (roughness floor 0.7, metalness ceiling 0.15, no clearcoat, and the albedo relationship to the frame), red-proved three ways including one that isolates the relationship pin from the hex pin.

**Bounds.** It pins this one feature's presence, placement, proportions and material. The general class — a surface whose features were never enumerated — is bounded by the loop's audit: only surfaces with a reference photo get compared, and only features a person thought to look for get enumerated. The lesson is the loop's own rule in `AGENTS.md` ("claiming fidelity to a real object requires a feature-by-feature audit"), not the test.

## 2026-09-16 — edge parts measured from a rail that was 0.36 mm too wide

**Symptom.** In the iPhone 17 Pro viewer every edge detail floated off the phone: the button pills hovered beside the frame with a dark gap behind them, the speaker and mic mouths sat 0.26 mm below the bottom edge as black pins, and the antenna ribbons hung 0.38 mm off the corners. In the profile shots the pills also rendered near-black and speckled with a diagonal hatch.

**Investigation.** `src/iphone/parts.test.ts` did not exist, so this started as a throwaway measurement of the built geometry (parts built with stub materials, real `Box3`s and real vertices):

- `slabGeometry({ width: 10, bevel: 1 })` spans ±6.00, i.e. `ExtrudeGeometry` applies the chamfer *outside* the shape handed in; the end faces are that outline and the slab's middle stands `bevel` proud.
- `buildHousing` hands it a shape pre-shrunk by `BODY.bevel`, so the frame's own `Box3` is exactly x ±35.950, y ±75.000, radius 11.5 — `BODY.width / 2`, `BODY.height / 2`, `BODY.radius`.
- `RAIL` read 36.31 / 75.36 / 11.86: the chamfer counted a second time.
- Measured against the real wall, the action pill spanned x[-36.83, -36.31] (0.36 mm clear of the body), its seam plate x[-36.83, -36.77] (0.82 mm clear), a speaker mouth y[-75.38, -75.26] (0.26 mm below the edge), and an antenna ribbon sat on a corner arc of 11.88 instead of 11.5.
- The pill's outer face and its seam's outer face were the *same plane*, both at ±36.83. Two coplanar faces fight for the same depth samples, and the near-black seam won often enough to read as a black, speckled pill — the diagonal hatch was that fight.

**Root cause.** Round 3 corrected the bevel direction and then applied it one time too many: `BODY.width / 2 + BODY.bevel` is the *shape's* outline, not the wall the shape's chamfer grows out to. Nothing caught it because nothing measured the built geometry against the surface it claims to sit on — every part and its expectation came from the same wrong constant.

**Gate.** `src/iphone/parts.test.ts`, `describe('the edge contract against the built geometry')`:

- the frame's own `Box3` must equal `RAIL` on all four sides — this is the *anchor*, and it is the check that has to exist first: parts are built *from* `RAIL`, so a wrong `RAIL` moves the measurement and the expectation together and every per-part bound still passes. Measured against the frame's real silhouette, it cannot.
- every edge part's outermost point must land on the rail plus its own documented stand-off, within `ATTACHED_SLACK` in either direction. Too far out is the floating pill; too far in is a pill buried in the frame, which is just as wrong and just as invisible.

Red proof, each run against the gate with that one mutation and then reverted:

- `RAIL` restored to `…/ 2 + BODY.bevel`: `puts the frame's own silhouette exactly on RAIL` →
  `expected 35.95000076293945 to be close to 36.31, received difference is 0.35999923706054915, but expected 0.0005`
- a pill placed on the 0.36-wide rail while `RAIL` is correct (the round-3 float, reproduced): `seats every control pill…` →
  `action-button stands 0.8800 mm past the rail; its documented stand-off is 0.52 mm: expected 0.8800018310546847 to be less than or equal to 0.53`
- antenna straps sunk 0.5 mm into the frame (the buried direction): `lays every antenna strap on the rail contour` →
  `antenna-band stops 0.4800 mm short of the rail, buried in the body (nearest vertex -0.4800 mm short): expected -0.47999649396991906 to be greater than or equal to -0.05`

**Caught on the gate's first run** (a real defect the eye had not reached): the plateau microphone window was 0.67 mm across inside a 0.75 mm hole, so the frame's bright aluminum back face was visible down that bore. Same class — a part sized against a number instead of against the opening it has to cover. Covered by `it('lines each cavity with a window at least as wide as the hole, recessed behind its bezel')`, which failed with `mic-hole is narrower than the 0.75 mm hole it lines: expected 0.6700000166893005 to be greater than or equal to 0.7`.

**Bounds.** The gate covers the XY silhouette of the parts listed in `parts.test.ts` — housing, front glass, plateau (with the lens stack), back panel, controls, antennas, bottom/top ports. It did not see `buildBack`'s logo or MagSafe ring: the logo was ungated here only because it then needed a DOM. Both now have node gates of their own (`logo.test.ts`'s placement describe, and `parts.test.ts`'s MagSafe describe, added 2026-09-17), and the MagSafe gate records that the ring's invisibility rests on a tessellation accident rather than on its intended stand-off. This entry also does not see `scene.ts`, `views.ts` or anything the renderer does.

## 2026-09-16 — back-facing slabs built from their outer plane

**Symptom.** In profile views the camera plateau read as a bar detached from the back of the phone with a dark gap between them, and in the top view as a shadowed step behind the body's top rail. Every lens sat at the bottom of a deep black bore instead of standing proud of the plateau in a polished ring.

**Investigation.** Measured `buildPlateau`'s plate: z[-8.470, -6.375], where `dims.ts` documents z[-6.375, -4.28]; the lens stack occupied z[-6.375, -3.175], i.e. entirely inside the frame. `slabGeometry` takes the slab's *largest* Z and grows towards -Z, and the plateau call site passed `PLATEAU.zOuter` (-6.375), the outer face. The back panel had the same error one step smaller: z[-5.575, -4.975] against a documented [-4.375, -4.975], leaving it floating 0.6 mm off the frame with a dark gap around its edge.

**Root cause.** `slabGeometry`'s parameter was named `outerZ` and read as "the outer surface" by every back-facing call site, while the function means "the maximum-Z face". On the front those coincide; on the back they are the two ends of the slab.

**Gate.** `describe('the documented Z planes')` in `src/iphone/parts.test.ts` asserts the plateau's plate against `PLATEAU.zOuter` / `zInner`, the panel against `PANEL_INNER_Z` / `PANEL_FACE_Z`, the cover glass against the frame's front face and `GLASS_FRONT_Z`, and each lens ring against `CAMERA_PLANE - LENS_RING_PROUD`. The parameter is now named `maxZ`, `dims.ts` carries the plane table with the rule for back-facing parts, and `slabGeometry`'s own contract comment says which plane a back-facing part hands it.

Red proof: with `maxZ: PLATEAU.zOuter` restored, `builds the camera plateau between the planes dims.ts documents` →
`expected -8.470000267028809 to be close to -6.375, received difference is 2.0950002670288086, but expected 0.0005`.

**Bounds.** Z planes only, and only for the parts the node test can build. A part buried *inside* another (invisible but present) passes, as do defects that are purely material or lighting.

## 2026-09-16 — Apple logo mirrored on the back (user-reported)

**Symptom.** The user, orbiting the live page: "the apple logo on the back is backwards." Five rounds of preset-view screenshots had all shipped with it — the logo was looked at in every back view and the chirality was never checked against a reference, because the inspection compared *overall read*, not individual features.

**Investigation.** A 1:1 crop of the back view (`.shots/audit-logo.png`) shows the bite on the LEFT and the leaf top-LEFT; every reference (Apple newsroom, GSMArena) has the bite on the right when the back is viewed upright. `appleLogoGeometry` applies `geometry.scale(scale, -scale, 1)` — a Y mirror that corrects SVG's y-down axis — which leaves the logo reading correct from +Z, but the back panel it is mounted on faces -Z, so the viewer sees the mirror image. The file's own header claimed "the bite on the right when the logo is seen from behind" — intended, never verified.

**Root cause.** One missing mirror: correcting the SVG's vertical flip without accounting for the panel facing -Z. The comment asserted the visual outcome and no render was ever checked for it.

**Gate.** `logo.test.ts` (round 6 split it out of `parts.test.ts`): the logo's leaf-cluster centroid — the vertices in the topmost 10 % of the inlay's Y range — must sit at world x < 0. The back view shows world −X on the right of frame (see `views.ts`'s axis note), so x < 0 is where the real leaf sits. The band is a fixture, not the leaf's own extent: on the 14 mm gate inlay the measured extents are body y [−7.000, +3.650] and leaf y [+3.656, +7.000], so the band is the inlay's topmost 1.4 mm — 10 % of 14 — and the leaf is 3.344 mm tall, so what the band catches is the leaf's tip and the two coincide only there. The gate would still measure the leaf if the two ever separated, and the test asserts separately that the band holds the leaf's tip. The SVGLoader import that made this class ungatable in node was replaced by a self-contained path reader, and the placement was extracted into a pure `logoLayout()` that the test drives with `mirrorX: false` to prove the check can fail.

Red proof: `the leaf cluster's centroid is at x = 2.154 (670 vertices): it is on the +X half, which a back view shows on the LEFT: expected 2.154408942763485 to be less than 0`.

Two further assertions pin what that band is made of, and both were rewritten in round 6 because they had been measuring the file's `maxY` — which, SVG y growing downwards, is the part's *bottom*: the two contours must not overlap in file Y (the leaf's `maxY` at or above the body's `minY`), and the body's crown — the file's `minY`, the end the Y mirror turns into its highest point — must land below the band's floor at +5.600 finished. Measured, the crown is at +3.650 and the leaf spans +3.656…+7.000; the isolation had been asserted nowhere, while the old crown check read `bodyExtents.maxY` and so reported exactly −7.000, the inlay's own bottom. Red proofs, each run and reverted: lifting the body's whole contour 100 file units → `the leaf's file bottom is y = 139.0 and the body's file top is y = 39.0: the two contours overlap, so the top band is not the leaf's alone: expected 138.9789276123047 to be less than or equal to 39.03601837158203`; lifting the crown to file y = 60 → `the body's crown lands at y = 6.124 in the finished inlay, above the band's floor at 5.600 — the top tenth the centroid measures holds body vertices: expected 6.12375976936939 to be less than 5.6`.

**Bounds.** Chirality of this one part. The parser reads M/m, C/c, Q/q, L/l and Z; a path using arcs or shorthand curves would need it extended. The inlay's size, position and depth are now gated too (`logo.test.ts`'s placement describe, 2026-09-17: height, centre, embed depth, and containment inside the panel's built footprint with stated margins); the leaf's *shape* and the outline's proportions remain verified visually, since nothing here has a reference outline to compare against.

## 2026-09-16 — USB-C port pierces the full body depth (user-reported)

**Symptom.** The user: "why is the usbc port on the back instead of at the bottom?" — a dark slot reads at the bottom centre of the back silhouette (and a notch from the front), where the real phone shows unbroken aluminum; the port should only read from the bottom edge.

**Investigation.** `.shots/audit-usbc-back.png` shows the slot at the back silhouette. `slabGeometry` pushes the slot into `shape.holes` and extrudes through the *whole* slab, so the cut pierces front face to back face; the "port-cavity" block spans z −4.2…+4.2 and the "port-tongue" block z −1.9…+1.9 sits *entirely inside* the solid cavity block — dead geometry, never visible from any angle. On the real phone the port is a blind recess opening on the bottom edge only. (Those two spans are the round-5 build this entry was written against; on the final tree the liner spans z ∈ [−4.3, +4.3] and the tongue z ∈ [−3.0, +4.1], y ∈ [−75.02, −74.40], visible through the floor's aperture.)

**Root cause.** A 2D-extrude can only make through-holes, and the through-cut was accepted as "a real hole … is what makes the port read as an opening" (the parts.ts comment) without ever looking at the port from the front or back.

**Found on the way, by measurement rather than by reading:** the slot hole had never opened the rail properly either. `slabGeometry` pre-shrinks the shape by `BODY.bevel`, so its own bottom line at the end faces is 0.36 mm above the rail; a hole posed *on* the rail was clipped by the triangulator to a 6.7 × 1.0 mm lens. The cut is now held `PORT_CUT_INSET` above the shape's bottom line so the full 8.4 × 3.2 mouth is inside the outline and actually cut, with its corners held to `PORT_CUT_RADIUS` (0.24) rather than the mouth's documented 1.1 — see `dims.ts`.

**The first fix was incomplete.** Round 6 closed the through-cut and its gates stayed green while three defects remained, and an adversarial review of the built geometry found all three:

- a **see-through slot**: a 0.65 mm band of the mouth's 3.2 mm — y ∈ [−72.496, −71.85] — covered by no part at all, so a ray along −Z crossed the phone's whole 8.75 mm depth. The gate meant to catch that compared the two shell plates' extreme Z with the frame's faces: both plates touched their face, so it was green, and nothing ever asked what the two plates left *between* them.
- an **unreachable tongue**: 0 of 7056 upward rays through the mouth met the tongue first, because the liner's floor had its top face on the rail's bottom face and covered the opening.
- a **Y-coplanar pair**: the liner's floor top and the rail's bottom face were both y = −75.0000, a gap of exactly 0.0000 — and the coplanarity gate only ever compared Z planes, so it could not see the axis the fight lived on.

**Gate.** Seven, in `port.test.ts` and `parts.test.ts`, all measuring the built geometry rather than the constants the parts are placed with. Every count below was measured on the round-6 final tree by this slice; three earlier slices reported different figures for the same gates, and none of theirs is carried over.

- **the through-cut, swept** (`seals the through-cut along Z at every point of the mouth`): a ray along −Z and one along +Z at every sample of the mouth's *documented* 8.4 × 3.2 rectangle — a 25 × 25 grid over x ∈ [−4.2, 4.2] and y ∈ [−75, −71.8], through the housing and the bottom assembly, 625 rays each way. Measured: **0 open samples of 625**. The sample count is asserted too, because an empty grid would pass this vacuously. Its red state is the 0.65 mm band above — reproducing it needs a shell plate removed from `port.ts`.
- **each shell plate against the face it hides** (`closes the through-cut at both of the frame's faces`): the old gate, kept — a plate that stops short of its face is a different defect from a gap between the plates. The tolerance is a literal 0.05 mm against the frame's own face, and each plate must also span the mouth's width.
- **the Y planes** (`keeps every port Y plane off the rail's and off each other`): every Y-facing plane of every port part against the rail's bottom face and against each other, skipping pairs whose boxes do not overlap in the X-Z plane (slack 1e-4). Measured: **43 overlapping pairs, smallest separation 0.0080 mm**, between `port-shell-front-high` and `port-cavity-ceiling-low`, against a literal threshold of 0.005; the floor's top face must sit *below* the rail's bottom face rather than level with it. Red proof, re-run: `PORT_POCKET.liner.drop = 0` → `rail-bottom and port-cavity-high are 0.0000 mm apart on Y — a coplanar pair: expected 0 to be greater than or equal to 0.005`.
- **the tongue's sightline** (`gives the tongue a sightline through the mouth`): rays launched at every sample of the mouth's documented rectangle in X and the body's 8.75 mm depth in Z (21 × 21 = 441, from y = −200, straight up) and from 40 mm outside the front face along (0, sin θ, cos θ) at 10°, 17° and 30° below the bottom face, first hit among the bottom assembly and the housing. Measured: **111 of 441 meet the tongue first** straight up (194 the frame's metal, 136 the liner's floor); **55 at 10°**, **90 at 17°** — the angle `views.ts`'s `bottom` preset looks along, worked from its `[0.06, −0.3, 0.95]` direction — and **156 at 30°**. The tongue is therefore visible well past "a few degrees": the 249/441 one slice reported is not reproducible on this tree, and neither is that claim. The old build's 0 hits is this class's red proof.
- **no two port Z faces on one plane** (`leaves no two port surfaces on one Z plane`, the gate the class was first seen on): measured **38 overlapping pairs, smallest separation 0.0200 mm** (`port-shell-front-front` against `frame-front` — `PORT_INSET` itself). Red proof, re-run: `PORT_INSET = 0.001` → `port-shell-front-front and frame-front are 0.0010 mm apart — a coplanar pair: expected 0.0009999275207519531 to be greater than or equal to 0.005`.
- **the containment bound is a literal** (`keeps the port cavity and its tongue inside the body`): 0.03 mm, not the implementation's own `PORT_POCKET.liner.thickness`. The earlier bound was built from the constant the part is placed with, so any thickness at all passed and a 0.5 mm lip under the phone would have gone green.
- **every block is solid, and the floor spans the mouth** (`builds the liner and the shell from blocks that are solid and span the mouth`, new in round 6 slice I): every mesh under `port-shell`, `port-cavity` and `port-cavity-ceiling`, plus the tongue (all six of the file's `blockGeometry` calls and both shell plates), must be positive in all three axes and at least the minimum its own comment documents — shell plates ≥ 8 × 3 × 0.1 mm, the ceiling ≥ 8 × 0.1 × 8, each floor block ≥ 1 × 0.01 × 0.45, the tongue ≥ 1 × 0.1 × 1 — as literals with the built values in the comments, not the constants the parts are placed with. The floor's four blocks together must cover the mouth's footprint in plan (8.4 mm across by the liner's 8.6 mm z reach, 25 × 25 = 625 samples) except an aperture that lies inside the tongue's own footprint; and on a 21 × 21 = 441-ray grid 0.05 mm inside that footprint every sightline from below must come back with the liner's floor or the tongue as its first hit — measured **216 floor, 225 tongue, 0 ceiling, 0 misses**. Red proof, re-run by restoring the loop's original un-ordered min/max X: `port-cavity-floor[0] measures 0.0020 mm in x where it is documented at 1.00 mm or more — a block collapsed towards zero is a surface that is not there: expected 0.002000093460083008 to be greater than or equal to 1`; with the extent assertion stood down, the same mutation gives `the liner's floor leaves the mouth's footprint uncovered at (-4.20, -4.30), (-4.20, -3.94), … — a sightline there reaches past it`, and then `a sightline from below crosses the liner's floor without meeting it at (-4.15, -3.83)=port-cavity-ceiling, (-4.15, -3.40)=port-cavity-ceiling, …`.

**Bounds.** The visibility gate fixes the rays, not the render: the counts are taken over the mouth's own footprint and at 0°, 10°, 17° and 30° below the phone's bottom face, and which of them reach the tongue is decided by whether they clear the rail's lip rather than by the tongue's own extent. The `bottom` preset in `views.ts` looks along `[0.06, −0.3, 0.95]`, 17.5° below the phone's horizontal plane and inside the sampled band; `scene.ts` sets no polar-angle limits, so a user's own orbit can go lower still, and how the port reads from the presets is otherwise a render's question — checked in this slice by sweeping `port`, `back` and `front` through `scripts/sweep-iphone.mjs` and reading the frames at native resolution: the front and back silhouettes stay unbroken over the port's x-range, and the bottom mouth reads as a dark recess whose liner floor is continuous around the aperture rather than a slot through the body. The pre-fix frame's recess reads black down its left flank, where the sightline reached the ceiling; a pixel diff of the two identically cropped frames puts every changed pixel in a strip down that flank (31,206 of 1,088,000 differ). The block-extent gate reads each mesh's bounding box, so it cannot see a block that is solid but displaced, and the plan-coverage claim is about the boxes' footprint rather than the plates' rounded corners (0.22 mm), where the shell plate behind the cut closes the sightline instead.

**The collapsed liner rail — found by the previous review, fixed here.** `port-cavity`'s left side rail measured 0.002 mm wide instead of 1.2: `port.ts`'s floor loop passed `side * mouthHalfWidth` as the *minimum* X for `side = -1`, so `blockGeometry` received a 1.2 mm negative width. `BoxGeometry` is symmetric in its width and `roundBoxVertices` then shrinks the box to its rounding radius, so the block was built without complaint and collapsed to x ∈ [−3.601, −3.599]. The group's own box was x ∈ [−3.601, 4.200] where the mouth is ±4.2, the mouth's left flank x ∈ [−4.2, −3.0] had no liner floor under it at all, and a sightline from below met the ceiling 3.2 mm up instead — 49 of the 625 upward samples over the footprint, against 0 on the fixed tree. The Y- and Z-plane gates stayed green through all of it, because each skips a pair whose boxes do not overlap in plan. The loop now orders each rail's two X ends per side, and the block-extent gate above is what will catch the whole class next time; the frames at `.shots/sweep/port-pre.png` and `port-post.png` show the difference from the same camera — a black left flank in the first, a continuous lit liner band in the second.

## 2026-09-16 — camera plateau edge is a machined step, not the forged roll (user-reported)

**Symptom.** The user: "the camera bump has a ramp on its edge instead of being immediately raised." On the real phone the bump's perimeter rises in a tight, smooth forged roll that also wraps over the phone's top edge; the model had a flat 2.1 mm vertical wall with a 0.18 mm chamfer — a machined step.

**Investigation.** A throwaway vitest probe (`wall-probe.test.ts`, deleted after) measured the built plateau: bottom wall vertical within 0.024 mm, top wall vertical, face-perimeter normals clean — i.e. the model was *too* machined, not too soft. Apple's close-up (`.shots/audit-ref-wall.png`), GSMArena gsmr-019/009 (the roll and the wrap over the top edge visible), and iPhone Mania's "段差が滑らか" all show the real profile: no flat wall band anywhere, the rise happening over a short horizontal run, and the raised surface rolling continuously over the phone's top.

**Root cause.** The plateau was built by `slabGeometry`, whose cross-section is definitionally flat-wall + chamfer; nobody had compared the *profile* against the real part at macro range.

**Gate.** In `plateau.test.ts` — the forged-roll gates moved there when `parts.test.ts` was split in round 6 — red-proved each: (a) the shoulder profile carries no flat vertical wall segment taller than 0.5 mm anywhere around the perimeter (`tallestWallSegment` over the sampled profile at four perimeter sites) — red: `top edge: the profile carries a 1.905 mm vertical wall — that is a step, not a roll`; (b) the bump's visible material reaches within 0.25 mm of the phone's top edge across |x| < 20 — red: `the surface at the top edge backs off to -4.652 — a band of the frame's back face is reachable behind it`; its second measurement was re-expressed in round 6 slice I, because the filter that picks the *visible* crest read `pz > BODY.halfDepth` — a sign that excludes nothing at all on a back-facing part — and so measured the buried base ring at 0.170 mm short instead of the vertex standing outside the frame's back face at 0.233. Both sit inside the 0.25 mm bound, which is why the gate stayed green while measuring the wrong surface; (c) coplanarity against the frame *as built*, by parity ray-casting: the base ring must be ≥ 0.02 mm inside the frame's surface (a bound derived from `RAIL` alone misses the frame's own 0.36 mm bevel, which is exactly how the first iteration hung its base in mid-air) — red: `the base ring comes within 0.0000 mm of the frame's surface — a ring on the surface is a coplanar pair, and one outside it hangs in the air`; (d) the plateau covers the bar, not the whole back — red: `the plateau reaches down to y = -74.83, past the bar's own bottom edge`; (e) the profile is monotone and never overshoots the base's own depth — red: `the profile overshoots to -4.246, past the base's own depth`; (f) the panel's top edge rises ≥ 2 mm from its centre to its sides — red: `the panel's top edge at x = 28 is only 0.000 mm above its centre`; (g) the ring crown's flat land is ≥ 60 % of the band — red: `the crown's flat land is 0.073 mm of a 1.45 mm band` (the first version of this gate read the implementation's own constant, so the mutation that set it to 0.05 passed — the bound is now a literal).

**The gate class the first iteration was blind to.** Its four original gates all measured the roll's cross-section, and a "plateau" swept around the *rail's whole outline* — a full-back plate — passed every one of them. Gate (d) exists because a cross-section gate cannot see extent. The same iteration also shipped a face cap facing +Z (invisible from the back) and a self-intersecting panel outline, both of which passed all gates and were caught only by rendering. Hence the round's process rule: a geometry worker renders its own work (`.shots` sweep) and inspects it before handoff.

**Bounds.** Shoulder profile, top reach, base attachment, footprint, panel rise and crown slope — all on the built plateau/panel. The roll's exact radius is set visually against Apple's close-up; the reflection band it produces (the metal mirroring the studio backdrop across a 2 mm sweep) is a materials/scene matter, not gated.
