# Devlog

## 2026-09-16 — iPhone 17 Pro viewer page

Added `iphone.html` and `src/iphone/`: a procedural iPhone 17 Pro (1 unit = 1 mm, 150.0 × 71.9 × 8.75, corner radius 11.5) with a live colour dropdown for Cosmic Orange, Deep Blue and Silver, and eight named camera presets behind `?view=` for the screenshot matrix. Gates: `npm run typecheck`, `npm run lint` (zero warnings), `npm test` (20 existing tests), `npm run build` all green.

### What a later session should know

- **`chrome --screenshot` with `--virtual-time-budget` cannot capture this page.** Every frame came back blank white: the flag captures before the WebGL scene has presented. `scripts/capture-shots.mjs` drives the same headless Chrome over the DevTools protocol, polls `window.__shotReady`, and only then calls `Page.captureScreenshot`. Use it, not the `--screenshot` flag. `scripts/shoot-iphone.ps1` originally used the flag and produced blank shots; it was later rewired to call `scripts/capture-shots.mjs` and is now the matrix entry point, so the flag itself stays the thing to avoid.
- **Framing is derived, not chosen.** Each preset is a stand-off `distance` plus a `frameHeight`, and the field of view falls out of those two. The first version fed a focal length through a 24 mm sensor, which produced fields of view of 102° and 153°; `OrbitControls` then clamped the distance to `minDistance` and every preset silently became the same 380 mm shot. Nothing about that was visible in the code — only in the render.
- **Preset targets are in scene space.** The phone stands from y = 0 (the floor) to y = 150. Targets written as `[0, 0, 0]` put the camera 75 mm below the body's centre and pushed most of the phone out of frame: the first hero shot showed one corner at 3.75× scale.
- **`Box3.setFromObject` bakes the world matrix into its result.** Measuring the model and then rotating it yields a box describing geometry that has already moved, and it is stale-matrix shaped: the numbers look plausible and are wrong. Set the transform first, then measure.
- **The port cut-out carved 20 mm off the phone.** `ExtrudeGeometry` holes are positioned relative to the shape, and the original offset pushed a 14 mm cut-out up into the middle of the body, so the phone measured 170 mm tall. There is now a diagnostic recipe for this: build the phone with the root's transform cleared, then read each part's `Box3` and assert it lies inside the body.
- **A `no-unsafe-assignment` lint error was a real type-resolution fact.** `@types/three` declares `Mesh<TGeometry = BufferGeometry, TMaterial = Material | Material[]>` such that a namespace import narrows every mesh to `Mesh<any, any, any>`. `src/iphone/types.ts` names the parameters and provides `isPhoneMesh`; without it, `node.geometry` and `node.material` are `any` and the strict rules fire on ordinary code.
- **The back panel's apparent seam was the MagSafe ring's contact shading.** A centre-line `gl.readPixels` sweep of the back view showed a smooth monotonic gradient (142 → 132) with a step at the ring's edge from the ring's own soft shadow. Recording this because the same "seam" was chased through three wrong hypotheses (extrude winding, misplaced slab, protruding cavity) before being measured.

## 2026-09-16 — round 3: every edge defect was a part measured from the wrong surface

Round 2's screenshots showed eight mechanical defects, and seven of them were the same mistake in different places: a part placed from `BODY.width / 2` or `BODY.height / 2`, which is the frame's *chamfered* outline, not its side wall. `slabGeometry` builds the bevel outside the shape it is given, so the flat wall every edge feature sits on is one `bevel` (0.36 mm) further out. `RAIL` in `dims.ts` now names that wall (`x`, `y`, `radius`, corner centres) and the buttons, antenna straps and edge ports key off it. The Camera Control pill had been buried inside the frame and the antenna blocks floated a millimetre over the corners for exactly this reason.

> **Correction, round 4: the paragraph above is wrong about which surface is the wall.** `buildHousing` hands `slabGeometry` a shape already pre-shrunk by `BODY.bevel`, so the wall the chamfer grows out to is the nominal outline — exactly `BODY.width / 2`, `BODY.height / 2`, `BODY.radius`. `RAIL` counted the bevel a second time and every part keyed off it floated 0.36 mm clear of the phone. Measured, not reasoned: the housing's own `Box3` is x ±35.950, y ±75.000. See the round-4 section.

What else a later session should know:

- **The lens barrel's floor capped the lens mouth.** `lens-barrel` and `lens-floor` were placed at `CAMERA_PLANE - x`, on the camera's side, so a flat disc covered every lens and hid the front element, the pupil and the stepped shoulder. Every lens rendered as a coin with a hole in it — the "slate grey coin" of the defect report — and no material change could fix it. They now sit at `CAMERA_PLANE + x`, behind the glass. The fix was found by raycasting the stack in Node from the camera direction and printing the hit order; the first hit was the floor, 1.1 mm in front of the plateau's face.
- **`domeGeometry`'s `sphereRadius` argument is not the sphere's radius.** It is the rise a cap of *that* radius needs for a sphere of the derived radius, so two caps only share one surface if each argument is solved for the same sphere. The pupil was a tighter cap than the glass it was supposed to float over, and all but its central millimetre was buried. `capParameter` in `lens.ts` does the solve.
- **`domeGeometry`'s rim wall defaulted to the full sphere radius.** At the glass's radius that is a 4.15 mm cylinder standing out of the plateau, and at the pupil's an 11.6 mm one: oblique views showed a blue pillar instead of a lens. It is now an explicit, short skirt.
- **The buttons were dark because the studio's two side softboxes faced outwards.** `studioEnvironment` places six emissive panels, and both ±X ones had a rotation that pointed them away from the cube's centre, so they were culled out of the PMREM environment entirely. Every surface whose normal lies along ±X — the rail, and every button pill on it — mirrored the dull grey shell behind the gap instead. The pills now share the frame's own material (`aluminum.clone()`), and the panels face the room.
- **`leanOffset` is for parts posed in floor space, not for children of the leaning root.** The Dynamic Island applied it while being a child of the rotated body, which pushed it 8 mm off the cover glass, where it read as a black blob floating at the screen's edge. The island's plane is simply the glass's own `outerZ` plus 0.05.
- **A tube through an edge is always a pin.** The speaker, bottom-mic and top-mic bores were open tubes centred on the edge line, so they stuck out 3.7–7 mm and rendered as black pins under the bottom edge and a black pill on the top edge. They are now flush dark discs (`BORE_PROUD = 0.02`), and the USB-C cavity sits a millimetre inside its cut-out with a lighter steel tongue.
- **Inspection tools used this round** (scratch, under the ignored `.shots/`): a dependency-free PNG crop/upscale tool, a pixel-line sampler, and a DevTools-protocol reader for `window.__iphone.debug()`. The last one is the cheap way to read the model's real bounds, which is how the button pills were confirmed to land on `RAIL.x + proud` (world min/max x = ∓36.83).
- **Hero framing was left alone.** Measured from the shot, the body already fills ~84 % of the frame height (the preset's `frameHeight` is 162 with `fitBody`), so the round-3 stretch note asking for ~75 % describes a *looser* frame than the one that ships. Nothing was changed rather than guessing which number was meant.

## 2026-09-16 — round 4: measure the surface before you place a part on it

Round 3's own report was the defect. It corrected the bevel direction and then applied it one time too many: `RAIL = BODY.width / 2 + BODY.bevel` is the *shape's* outline, not the wall that shape's chamfer grows out to. Every edge part keyed off the wrong table and floated clear of the phone — pills 0.36 mm out, bore mouths 0.26 mm below the bottom edge, antenna ribbons 0.38 mm off the corners. Seven of the eight round-4 defects were that one number, plus the chain of things it hid.

What a later session should know:

- **`RAIL` is measured, and the gate is what keeps it measured.** `parts.test.ts` first asserts the housing's own `Box3` equals `RAIL` on all four sides. That anchor has to come first, because the parts are built *from* `RAIL`: with a wrong `RAIL`, the geometry and the expectation move together and every per-part bound still passes. Proved by putting the old table back — only the anchor test goes red.
- **Place a part from the surface it touches, in both directions.** The gate now requires each edge part's outermost point to land on the rail plus that part's documented stand-off, within 0.05 mm either way: out is a floating part, in is a buried one. Both were real here.
- **Two faces on one plane is not a thin gap, it is a fight.** The button seam was given the pill's `outerX`, so a near-black seam face sat exactly coplanar with the pill's aluminum face. The pills rendered black and speckled (the diagonal hatch in the profile shots) and no material change would have fixed it. The seam now has its own stand-off (0.06 vs the pill's 0.52) and both extrude `EMBED = 0.1` back into the metal instead of ending on the wall.
- **`slabGeometry`'s plane is the slab's maximum Z.** Back-facing parts must hand it their *inner* face. The plateau passed `zOuter`, so the whole bar sat 2.095 mm behind its documented planes and the entire lens stack lived inside the frame. `dims.ts` now carries the plane table; the parameter is named `maxZ`.
- **A lathe profile that crosses back through itself is a notch.** `domeGeometry` took a sphere radius and a rise that did not solve to it, so each cap's rim sat 0.653 mm *below* its own base plane and the skirt folded back up through the cap. That fold was the notch at 6 o'clock on every lens ring; the sphere is now solved from radius and rise (`capSphere`), and the rim lands exactly on the base plane (proved by measuring the profile's z range: [-0.6, 0.45] for a 0.45 rise).
- **Stacking a tube, a floor disc and two step rings puts four surfaces on one radius.** The seams between them were the concentric ridges the closeup showed inside every lens. A cavity is now one continuous lathe profile (`profileLathe`).
- **A window must be wider than the hole it covers.** Every lens covers a bore through the plateau with its barrel; the mic window was 0.67 mm across inside a 0.75 mm hole, so the frame's bright aluminum back face was visible down that cavity. `parts.test.ts` caught this on its first run — it is the one defect in round 4 that no screenshot had reported.
- **Measuring the same numbers wrongly is the other half of this round.** Vertex positions are 32-bit floats, so a bound met exactly reads ~4 µm out and a 1e-6 tolerance fails on arithmetic; `Box3.setFromObject` refreshes the object's subtree but not the groups above it, so a lens ring measured without refreshing ancestors silently reports its local coordinates. Both are named in `parts.test.ts`'s header.
- **Contrast is measurable, and "looks darker to me" is not.** The antenna bands failed twice on material guesses. The working instrument: capture two arms that differ *only* in the antenna material (the control being the rail's own finish), diff the PNGs at native resolution to locate the band's exact pixels, then compare the band arm against the neighbouring rail pixels. Measured band-versus-rail luminance: 54.7 of 255 in the top view, 21.3 in the left profile, 36.6 in the bottom view. The first attempt (matte, 0.45 albedo) measured **4.0** in the top view — invisible again, and exactly the kind of "fixed" that a screenshot review at a different angle would have confirmed. Scratch: `.shots/w4*`, `output/w4-diff.mjs`.
- **The flash's emissive was the blowout.** `emissive: 0xfff2d6` at 0.85 rendered as a pure white disc under ACES, brighter than the studio's own softboxes. It is a window you look at, not a light: matte pale, no emissive.
